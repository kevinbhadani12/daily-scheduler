Every day, prepare a useful briefing covering the most important and genuinely new business developments from India and around the world.

Coverage: Monitor all major business sectors, including technology, AI, software, automation; startups, entrepreneurship, SaaS, new business ideas; retail, e-commerce, D2C brands, consumer products; banking, finance, the economy, financial services; manufacturing, automobiles, electric vehicles; real estate, construction, infrastructure; healthcare, pharmaceuticals, biotechnology; agriculture, food, FMCG; energy, oil, renewable energy, climate-related business; logistics, transportation, aviation, travel; education, media, entertainment, telecom; government policies, regulations, international trade, global business; company results, mergers, acquisitions, layoffs, hiring, expansion, and major leadership changes. Include other sectors when important news emerges.

Select up to 8 of the most important and genuinely new developments each day, depending on the availability of useful news. Prioritize events that could change an industry or business; new products, services, technologies, and business models; companies entering markets or changing strategies; funding, acquisitions, partnerships, and financial results; changes in customer behavior, competition, costs, jobs, and regulations; and credible business opportunities, emerging problems, and lessons. Include a balanced mix of Indian and international news. Avoid gossip, routine promotional announcements, rumors presented as facts, minor updates, and repetitive stories. Do not invent news to fill the briefing.

For every story, use this structure: (A) What happened? Explain in 2–4 short sentences, including company, people, place, date, and important numbers where relevant. (B) Explain it like I am new to this business: explain what the company or industry does, how its business works, and background needed. (C) Give a real-world everyday example. (D) Why does this matter? Explain effects on customers, employees, business owners, competitors, the industry, or economy. (E) How does the company make money? When relevant, explain its business model and how the development might affect revenue, costs, profits, or growth. (F) What can we learn? Share a practical lesson, opportunity, or risk. (G) Important terms: explain unfamiliar terms in one simple sentence each. (H) Sources: provide direct links to reliable sources, preferably official announcements, government publications, financial reports, and reputable news organizations; include publication dates where possible.

At the beginning, include today's date, the 3 most important developments, a short explanation of the overall business mood (what is growing, struggling, or changing), the most important India-specific development, and the most important global development.

At the end include: Business Model of the Day (explain one company, how it makes money, who pays it, major costs, and why customers choose it); Business Lesson of the Day (a practical lesson from a real company or event); Opportunity to Explore (one credible business opportunity, customer problem, or emerging trend, who may benefit, potential customers, and challenges; never guarantee speculative opportunities); New Vocabulary (3–5 useful English business words with simple definitions and example sentences); Quick Revision (3 short questions, with answers separately below).

Use simple, natural English for an intelligent beginner who may know nothing about the industry. Use short sentences and paragraphs. Expand abbreviations the first time. Avoid unnecessary jargon and complicated financial language. Use Indian examples and rupee values where relevant. Use small tables or simple diagrams when useful. Explain percentages, large numbers, and financial figures in context. Separate verified facts, company claims, expert opinions, and predictions. Mention uncertainty when reliable information is unavailable. Never fabricate statistics, sources, quotations, or announcements.

Aim for a 10–15 minute read. Explain fewer important stories clearly rather than listing many headlines without context. Compare with previous briefings to avoid repeating stories unless there is a meaningful new development. For continuing stories, explain what changed. The goal is to help me understand how businesses across different industries operate, make money, compete, grow, fail, and respond to changes in the world.
